Adjusted net income

Three of the sharpest thresholds in UK tax key off this one figure — and it is not your salary. People check their salary, miss the threshold, and find out in January.

What it is

Adjusted net income is your total taxable income from every source, minus a short list of deductions. It is not gross pay, not taxable pay, and not the number on your payslip. Someone on a £95,000 salary with a £10,000 bonus and some rental income has an adjusted net income well over £100,000, and will be treated accordingly.

ISAs are invisible to all three thresholds. Interest and dividends inside an ISA are not taxable income, so they never appear in adjusted net income. Outside one, the same money can push you over a cliff.

The three thresholds it decides

1 · The personal allowance taper — £100,000

Above £100,000 the personal allowance is withdrawn at £1 for every £2 of income, so it is gone entirely by £125,140. In that band the marginal rate is 60%, and 62% once National Insurance is counted. The 100k tax trap calculator shows what each extra thousand actually keeps, and the adjusted net income calculator works out where you sit.

2 · The High Income Child Benefit Charge — £60,000

From £60,000 a charge claws back Child Benefit, reaching 100% of it at £80,000. It is assessed on the higher earner's adjusted net income, not household income — so two people on £55,000 each pay nothing, while one on £85,000 loses all of it. The Child Benefit calculator puts a figure on it.

3 · The childcare cliff — £100,000

This is the brutal one, because it is a cliff and not a taper. Cross £100,000 by a single pound and you lose Tax-Free Childcare and the funded hours outright — not gradually. For a family using both, one pound of extra income can cost several thousand. The childcare cliff calculator and the Tax-Free Childcare calculator quantify it.

The cliff is why a pay rise can leave you worse off. A taper takes a share of the extra. A cliff takes the whole benefit the moment you cross. Stacked with the 60% band, a family crossing £100,000 can be materially poorer than before the raise — which is the one case where turning down money, or putting it in a pension, is straightforwardly correct.

The lever you control

Pension contributions are deducted from adjusted net income, which means a contribution can pull you back under a threshold. In the 60% band the effective cost of a £1,000 contribution is £400. Against the childcare cliff it can be better than free.

Gift Aid works the same way and is often forgotten: donations are grossed up and deducted, so £80 given is £100 off your adjusted net income.

Where the other income comes in

If the number that tips you over is not salary, these are the ones to check: dividends, self-employed profit, benefits in kind, a bonus landing in the wrong tax year, or a second job. A company car in particular adds its P11D value to the figure without ever appearing in your bank account.

For the whole picture on one salary, the take-home pay calculator runs tax, National Insurance, pension and student loan together, and the pay rise calculator shows how much of a raise survives a threshold.

Timing

Adjusted net income is measured over the tax year, 6 April to 5 April — not your employer's year and not the calendar. A bonus paid on 3 April counts in a different year from one paid on 8 April, which matters enormously if you are near a cliff.
